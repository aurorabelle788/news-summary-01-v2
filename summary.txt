World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026. Two days of US-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine ended in Abu Dhabi with no ceasefire, but agreement on the largest prisoner exchange of the war -- 1,000 to 1,500 on each side, Kyiv said -- as envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff headed to meet Vladimir Putin in Moscow (Reuters, NPR). Russia meanwhile struck six power substations in central Ukraine, and a market attack in the Odesa region killed three (Reuters). Nvidia's board met late on Wednesday to decide chief executive Jensen Huang's fate, the Wall Street Journal reporting it was preparing to oust him over two reported extramarital affairs; the New York Times reported a second affair that evening, and Nvidia shares fell more than 8% after hours (Reuters). The affair was earlier reported to have helped at least $7.5 billion of Nvidia chips reach Chinese firms, and a US senator had called for Huang to resign (The Information, Reuters). At COP31 in Antalya, leaders opened a two-day summit; UN chief Antonio Guterres warned slow climate action had put the world "on a path to destruction", with 77 heads of state due to speak, down from 116 at COP28, and the US absent for a second year (Reuters). The US Justice Department charged three Iran-backed operatives over about 20 plots in the US and Europe, including one at RAF Fairford, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moved to a military base after threats (New York Times). Wall Street rebounded: the S&P 500 rose 1.3% to 7,619, the Nasdaq 1.6% to 25,466 and the Dow 0.5% to 52,486, as CoreWeave jumped almost 25% on strong earnings; Europe's STOXX 600 hit a record high and the dollar a 19-month high (Investopedia, Reuters). The House voted 211-204 to send eight articles impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively blocking the effort (AP). British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer with a NATO unit near Gloucester on suspicion of spying for Russia; Moscow denied any link (CNN, AP).
